The Wichita Life App

Your list is large and engaged enough to make this realistic. The risk is scope. Fifteen features at launch would sink it. Pick one daily habit, launch that, and add the rest in phases.

Is this a crazy idea?

No. Building it all at once would be.

Most local apps fail because they can't get people to install them. You already have a big, engaged audience in one metro. A 49% open rate means about 22,000 people open your email every send. That's the hardest part of a city app, and you've already got it.

What can hurt you is being spread thin. Chat, a job board, and a business directory each need their own supply and moderation. Launch with the thing people will open daily: what's happening in Wichita today. Everything else gets added on top.

The name

Recommendation: “Wichita Life” in the app stores, with the subtitle “The Wichita App: news, events & perks.” This keeps the brand your 45K subscribers already know. “The Wichita App” works as a search phrase and a tagline, but it's generic and harder to trademark.

Your list, in the order to build it

Phase 1 · The daily habit

MVP · ~8–12 weeks

Goal: get subscribers to install the app and turn on notifications. Keep it to features you already produce content for.

  • coreToday feed: news pulled from RSS feeds (headline, source, and a link out) mixed with your own posts. This is how you replace their five other sources.
  • coreEvent calendar: filter by weekend, free, or family, and save events to get a reminder.
  • corePush notifications: one strong push a day at most, plus opt-in topics like giveaways, events, and breaking news.
  • coreGiveaways: enter inside the app and require notifications to be on. This will drive most of your installs.
  • coreNewsletter + evergreen content: pull posts and your archive from beehiiv. Signing up in the app also subscribes people to the email.
  • coreMeet: RSVPs for dinner club and hosted meetups, interest groups, and a community board where a moderator approves every post before it goes live. Moved up from later phases because community is core to the brand.
  • coreSpotlights: people and business features, published through the same beehiiv feed.

Phase 2 · Money & meeting up

~3–4 months after launch

Goal: bring in revenue that doesn't depend on newsletter ads, and turn app users into people who show up in person.

  • revenuePerks / rewards: a digital member card with offers people show at the counter. Businesses pay for featured perks.
  • revenueTickets: start with your own events, like dinner club. Selling other promoters' tickets means refunds and support you don't need yet.
  • coreScavenger hunts: scan QR codes at partner businesses to check in, with a prize at the end. Businesses pay to be a stop.
  • revenueBusiness directory: start small by listing businesses you've already featured, then sell upgraded listings.

Phase 3 · Reasons to stay

6–12 months

Goal: keep people coming back between newsletter sends.

  • coreGames: a daily Wichita trivia or word game with a streak and a leaderboard. It's cheap to build and gives people a reason to open the app every day.
  • revenuePremium: an “Insider” tier with early ticket access, extra giveaway entries, and premium perks. It can use beehiiv's paid tiers.
  • revenueJob board: jobs.wichitalifeict.com already exists. Pull its listings into the app and sell featured posts.
  • corePodcast / interviews: embed the audio. Only add it once you're publishing episodes regularly.

Hold for now

revisit once there's a real community
  • carefulOpen chat: the highest-moderation, highest-liability feature on the list. It feels empty with few users and gets toxic with many. If you want it, start with chats limited to one event or group, like a dinner club table.

How to build it

Recommended: custom app, borrowed back end

  • Expo (React Native): one codebase for iPhone and Android.
  • Supabase: accounts, events, perks, giveaway entries, and the database.
  • beehiiv: stays your content and newsletter system, with the app reading from it.
  • Stripe: payments for tickets and premium.
  • A scheduled job that reads your RSS feeds and removes duplicates every 15 minutes.

Faster, less control: app builders

  • White-label community or “city app” platforms can launch in weeks.
  • The trade-off is a monthly fee, less control over design, and your data sitting on their platform.
  • This is a good choice if you only want to test whether people will install it before investing.

What to watch

Risks

  • News rights: show headlines and short snippets that link out. Never republish full articles.
  • Your time: every feature needs content or moderation. Count the hours you'd spend per week, not just the build cost.
  • Too many pushes: people turn notifications off fast. Stick to one daily push and let people opt in to topics.
  • Empty sections: a thin directory or job board looks dead. Launch those only with real listings already in them.

90-day targets (proposed)

  • 5,000 installs, about 11% of your list
  • 60%+ of users with notifications turned on
  • 35%+ of users opening the app each week
  • At least 3 paying sponsors on in-app placements

Decisions for our next session

  1. Budget and timeline: do you want to hire a developer, use an app builder, or build it with Claude over several sessions?
  2. RSS sources: which 5–10 local news feeds should the Today feed pull from?
  3. Launch hook: what giveaway is big enough to get people to install on day one?
  4. Next step: should I build a clickable prototype of Phase 1 so you can test it with your dinner club?
